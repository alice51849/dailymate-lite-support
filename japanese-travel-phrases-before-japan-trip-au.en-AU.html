Japanese travel phrases before your Japan trip: what to practise first

Flying from Australia to Japan for the snow, the cities or a stopover? You do not need a whole course before you go. Practise a handful of Japanese travel phrases for the moments you will meet on day one, and learn the reply that usually comes back.

Which situations to practise first

Start with the situations that happen on every trip: airport security, hotel check-in, asking directions and ordering food. In DailyMate Lite these sit in the Travel topic, and Japanese is one of the 50 learning languages. The whole library has 8,400 original cue-and-reply pairs across 14 topics and 84 real-life situations, at Beginner, Intermediate and Advanced levels, but for a first trip the Travel topic at Beginner level is the place to begin.

Why learn the reply, not just your line

A phrase list teaches you what to say. The hard part is the next line: the question the hotel desk asks back, or the answer you get when you ask for directions. Each card in DailyMate Lite pairs a line you are likely to hear with a natural reply, so you practise whole exchanges rather than isolated words.

Hearing Japanese at a speed you can follow

Every phrase can be heard with matching on-device system voices, and you can adjust the speed. Slow a line down until you can catch it, say your reply out loud, then go back to normal speed. Progress, streaks and saved phrases are kept separately for Japanese, so another language you are learning does not get mixed in.

What is free, and is it a subscription?

In any learning language, including Japanese, the complete Travel topic at Beginner level is free. Other topics and levels stay visible as genuine previews. If you want more, a one-time lifetime unlock opens every topic, all three levels, the complete 8,400-pair library, unlimited saved phrases, interactive widgets, Apple Watch and App Intents. There is no subscription and no account to create.

A five-day plan before you fly

  1. Choose Japanese as your learning language and open the Travel topic at Beginner level.
  2. Day one: airport. Day two: hotel check-in. Hear the cue, say your reply aloud, compare it with the card.
  3. Day three: ordering food. Day four: asking directions.
  4. Put the widget on your Home Screen so the next phrase is there each time you pick up your phone.
  5. Day five: run through only the cards you saved because they felt hard.
