Travel phrases before your trip: four questions people ask first

Before a holiday, four questions about travel phrases come up first: how much is in it, can I hear it, what can I try for free, and is it a subscription. DailyMate Lite answers each of them plainly below.

How many phrases are there, and how many do I need?

DailyMate Lite has 8,400 original cue-and-reply pairs across 50 learning languages, 14 topics and 84 real-life situations, at Beginner, Intermediate and Advanced levels. You do not need all of them for a trip. For a first holiday, the situations you meet every day are enough: airport security, hotel check-in, asking directions and ordering food. Each card pairs a line you are likely to hear with a natural reply, so you learn what to say and how the conversation can continue, not just an isolated word.

Can I hear how each phrase sounds?

Yes. Every phrase can be heard with matching on-device system voices, and you can adjust the speed. Slow it down until you can catch the other person's line, say your reply out loud, then return to normal speed. Every language keeps its own progress, streaks and saved phrases, so you can practise the language of each destination separately.

What can I practise for free?

In any learning language, the complete Travel topic at Beginner level is free. That covers the basic travel situations most people need before a trip. Other topics and levels stay visible as genuine previews, so you can see what is there before deciding anything.

Is it a subscription?

No. A one-time lifetime unlock opens every topic, all three levels, the complete 8,400-pair library, unlimited saved phrases, interactive widgets, Apple Watch and App Intents. There is no subscription, no account to create, and no third-party advertising, analytics or tracking.

A one-week plan before you fly

  1. Choose the language of where you are going and open the Travel topic at Beginner level.
  2. Day one: airport and hotel. Hear the cue, say your reply aloud, compare it with the card.
  3. Day two: ordering food. Day three: asking directions and other everyday situations.
  4. Put the widget on your Home Screen so the next phrase is there when you unlock your phone.
  5. The day before you leave, run through only the cards you saved because they felt hard.

Every language keeps its own progress, streaks and saved phrases, so a second destination later in the year does not mix with the first.
